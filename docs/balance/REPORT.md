# Balance report

Generated by `tools/sim/run_balance.gd` (#6): 5000 seeded runs per strategy, 10 days each, same seeds for every strategy.

**The bot:** lands Super Perfect, Perfect, Good, OK and Miss equally often (20% each; a Miss lands $1.01–$3.00 off, over or under at random). It starts a pour 1.0 s after a car parks, plus up to 0.6 s per other hand-played pump. Cars take 3.0 s to drive in.
**The game:** today's numbers: 1 starting pump, $50 starting cash, rent $60 + $40 a day, a 400 L tank, fuel 80% of the pump price wholesale.

## What it says (Committee read, 2026-10-20)

1. **Nobody survives today's economy.** With an even split of tiers, the best strategy completes a 10-day run 3% of the time. Rent grows $40 a day (day 10: $420), while a pump keeps only about 20% of each sale plus tips.
2. **The economy sits on a knife edge.** Small changes flip it completely: rent +$30 a day takes "Always the cheapest upgrade" from 0% to 99%, and wholesale fuel at 70% takes "Pumps first" to 100%. Tune in small steps.
3. **Automation loses.** A self-serve pump pours at half speed, pays no tips and keeps about 17% of the sale after fuel and the card fee. Even at 75% speed it changes nothing. This contradicts the run-arc goal (mostly automated by a third of the run): self-serve needs a bigger margin, not just speed. (The two automation bots tie because they only ever afford one self-serve pump.)
4. **Filling the tank every night is a trap.** "Pumps first, but always fill the tank" builds half as many pumps as the just-in-time version. The fuel order is a real decision, as intended.
5. **When the economy is livable, expansion wins.** More pumps played by hand, or the cheapest upgrade each night, are the strongest routes. Marketing and service/vibe come second.

Caveats: the bot never gets better or worse, and it juggles five hand-played pumps at the same 20/20/20/20/20 split, which flatters hand-played stations. Just-in-time fuel still runs dry on some days.
