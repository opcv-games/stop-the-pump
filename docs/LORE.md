# GOD PUMP: the lore

> The world's bible (round 8). The newspaper (scripts/god/newspaper.gd), the regulars' lines
> (scenes/god/day/god_pump.gd, QUIPS), the intro and the ending (scenes/god/ui/cinematic.gd) all draw
> from it. Tone: tongue-in-cheek, warm, small-town Québec. English text, French names and quotes.

## The place
**Saint-Clin-Clin-des-Meuh-Meuh, Québec.** Population 412, and more cows than that (the Meuh-Meuh).
- **On the road:** a church with a silver steeple, the rang where the farms are, and Route 132.
- **The two stations:** your gaz bar, and **Chez Gaétan** across the road. Gaétan's prices are always a little higher.
- **The paper:** L'Écho de Saint-Clin-Clin, founded 1952, 25¢.

## The family
- **Dad** ran the gaz bar for thirty years. He only opened mornings. After 11, he went fishing.
  - Last spring he "passed away". The will left his only child THE GOD PUMP.
  - The truth: he faked it, to go fishing, and kept going.
  - He sends four postcards, signed "un pêcheur" (a fisherman):
    - **Day 10, Lac-à-la-Truite:** "The fish are biting."
    - **Day 20, Lac Saint-Jean:** "Not bad, kid."
    - **Day 30, Gaspésie:** "Your prices are too cheap."
    - **Day 40, Florida:** "Running out of worms. See you soon."
  - **Day 50:** he drives in, sunburnt, in the faded orange hatchback, a fishing rod out the window. "T'es un vrai gaz bar boy."
- **You,** the kid. The town watches you learn the pump, and wonders if you're single.

## The regulars (the bench by the door)
- **Ti-Guy:** short, red plaid flannel, a cap, a moustache; a dairy farmer.
  - Swears he saw Dad at Lac-à-la-Truite.
  - Takes bets on "the father's return."
  - His line: "Y'a du talent, le jeune!"
- **Mme Gagnon:** grey bun, glasses, a pink cardigan, a purse; writes the gossip column in L'Écho, "Les potins de Mme Gagnon".
  - Keeps every one of your headlines.
  - Has a niece, Sylvie. She's very nice.
- **Bertrand:** bald, big belly, suspenders; a retired mechanic and Dad's best friend.
  - Knows more than he says.
  - Buys a fishing rod and a bus ticket to Gaspésie around day 26.
- **Le Grand:** very tall, a leather jacket, a mullet, a beer; plays in Les Pistons (church basement, Thursdays).
  - Asks the bench: "Y'é-tu célibataire, lui?"
  - Steps out for a smoke.

## Around town
- **Me Tremblay, the notary:** read the will, nervously. Buys a lot of stamps. Declines to comment on "certain matters regarding the late owner."
- **Cousin Réjean:** works nights once you can pay him (HEURES).
- **Gaétan:** runs the station across the road. "Competition," he says, raising his prices.
- **The curé:** rings the bells at 11 sharp, "when he used to go fishing."
- **Pétroles St-Clin:** the fuel truck, number 31. Its prices only go up.

## Where the lore shows
- **The intro:** the will, Me Tremblay, the four regulars.
- **The regulars' quips** during the day, the rumours included.
- **The visitors (round 9, scripts/god/visitors.gd):** on set days someone drives in and chats with the regulars:
  - Gaétan (days 2, 18, 45), Sylvie (4, 23), Me Tremblay (6, 15, 34), cousin Réjean (8, 37), the curé (12, 26).
  - The mail carrier reads Dad's postcards out on days 10, 20, 30 and 40.
- **The menu screen's scrapbook:** what was last overheard, and the postcards so far.
- **The ending:** the postcards, Mme Gagnon gasps, Bertrand doesn't.
